\section{Planned Experimental Protocol: Continuous Profiles and Scale Transfer}
\label{sec:planned_profile_mup}

\paragraph{Status and scope.}
The recovered 30-run geometry pilot is reported in Fig.~\ref{fig:profile-geometry-pilot} using validation data only. Complete modern profile-confirmation, residual/ViT-bridge, and width-transfer result grids were not available in the recovered records; the protocol below specifies those studies and is not used to claim completed transfer results. Their purpose is to test whether the variational scheduling argument predicts a continuous ordering of profile shapes and whether the selected training hyperparameters transfer across width once optimizer-induced width dependence is removed.

\paragraph{Continuous profile family.}
Let \(x_\ell=(\ell+\tfrac12)/L\), for \(\ell=0,\ldots,L-1\), denote the cell-centered normalized depth. We will compare a capped power family
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 p_{\alpha,\mathrm{early}}(x_\ell)
   &=\min\!\left\{p_{\max},A_\alpha(1-x_\ell)^\alpha\right\},\\
 p_{\alpha,\mathrm{late}}(x_\ell)
   &=p_{\alpha,\mathrm{early}}(1-x_\ell),
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:planned_power_profiles}
\end{equation}
where \(A_\alpha\) is solved separately at each finite depth so that \(L^{-1}\sum_\ell p_\ell=\bar p\) exactly. The cases \(\alpha=0,1,2,4\) give constant, linear, quadratic, and more concentrated power profiles; early and late saturated steps provide the extremal comparators. Each early/late pair is an exact reversal and therefore has the same multiset of fields and the same leading propagation cost \(L^{-1}\sum_\ell h_\ell^a\), with \(a=1/2\) for smooth and \(a=1/3\) for kinked channels. It differs only in the regularization-reach ordering. This design separates the prediction that concentration preserves propagation from the prediction that early placement exposes more downstream computation to the mask. Primary comparisons preserve the exact finite-depth mean of the raw dropout probabilities \(p_\ell=1-\rho_\ell\), with \(p_\ell\leq0.20\). The exact one-step field \(h_\ell=\Delta(\rho_\ell)\) is retained as an analytic initialization diagnostic rather than a separate training cohort: in the zero-bias case it equals \(p_\ell\) exactly (for either activation), while at the selected biased ReLU initialization exact-field matching changes any quadratic-profile layer probability by at most \(3.59\times10^{-4}\). This makes the removed 60-run sensitivity grid mostly duplicate treatment rather than a useful empirical contrast.

The mechanistic comparison will relate every exact schedule to \(D_a=L^{-1}\sum_\ell h_\ell^a\) and to its implied correlation-depth proxy. The primary endpoint is final-epoch cross-entropy on the locked historical benchmark. Secondary endpoints are final benchmark accuracy, exact terminal validation loss, the mean validation loss over the final 10\% of epochs, normalized validation-loss area under the curve, profile-rank stability, and compute-to-baseline. Initialization, minibatch order, and dropout each use a separately recorded random stream; exact early/late reversal pairs share dropout common random numbers, while unrelated profile families remain independent. Profile and learning-rate selection will use a validation split; the locked historical benchmark test set from the original notebooks---which informed the published work and is therefore not a fresh holdout---will be evaluated only after the extension protocol is locked.

The profile study keeps the existing plain MLP at \(L=6\). A three-seed ReLU geometry pilot at \(\bar p=0.10\) scans \(\alpha=0,1,2,4\), both orientations, saturated steps, and the no-dropout control. Ten fresh paired seeds at \(\bar p=0.10\) then compare constant, quadratic-early/late, and step-early/late profiles at \(N=256\). The same five-profile confirmation is repeated with the smooth GELU activation at its near-critical initialization. Training uses fixed stratified \(4000/1000\) train/validation sets and the locked historical 5000-example benchmark test set; pilot trials keep the test metrics sealed, and confirmation trials evaluate the benchmark once at the final epoch.

As a deliberately separate architectural boundary test, a 30-run residual/ViT bridge uses 10 pre-norm residual blocks at width 128 and applies each block's dropout probability to both the attention and MLP residual branches. It preserves the result-generating small-ViT recipe's unit residual-branch scale, AdamW/cosine schedule, and global gradient-norm clipping at 1.0, and compares constant, quadratic-early/late, and step-early/late profiles with six paired seeds. This bridge is not a direct verification of the plain-MLP recursion and makes no Transformer-\(\mu\)P claim. Opposite early--late signs between the MLP and residual/ViT cohorts are prespecified as architecture-dependent effect modification, not as experimental failure. It also is not a reproduction of the separate ResNet-56 protocol, which uses 27 scheduled residual blocks, SGD, \(\bar p=0.05\), and no gradient clipping.

\paragraph{\(\boldsymbol{\mu}\)P width transfer.}
The experiments above use standard parameterization. A separate study will implement maximal-update parameterization (\(\mu\)P) and its associated zero-shot width-transfer protocol \cite{yang2022tensorprogramsv}. The hidden-layer critical initialization is retained, while the readout and AdamW parameter groups follow \(\mu\)P scaling. The shape models have widths \(64\) and \(128\); the tuning proxy has width \(128\); and the held-out targets have widths \(256,512,1024,\) and \(2048\), all at \(L=6\). The \(\mu\)P readout and the matched standard-parameterization control are both zero-initialized so that the parameterization, rather than readout initialization, is the controlled difference. A multiplicative cosine scheduler preserves all optimizer-group learning-rate ratios and reaches a final multiplier \(10^{-3}\).

Before training, three-seed coordinate checks across widths \(64\)--\(2048\), using the largest scanned learning rate \(3\times10^{-3}\) and at least two real updates, will verify that both hidden- and output-coordinate slopes against log width remain within \(\pm0.1\). The check must also exceed a prespecified nonzero update threshold, so an effectively unchanged model cannot pass. At the width-\(128\) proxy we scan \(\eta\in\{10^{-5},3\!\times\!10^{-5},10^{-4},3\!\times\!10^{-4},10^{-3},3\!\times\!10^{-3}\}\) with three seeds, select by mean final validation cross-entropy, and freeze the selected learning rate. Leave-one-seed-out selections must agree with the full-cohort choice; otherwise a prespecified validation-only full-grid extension with fresh seeds \(3\)--\(5\) is completed before transfer. Ten paired seeds then transfer that setting and the five confirmation profiles to every held-out width. Transfer regret is measured against target-side diagnostic sweeps at all four target widths, run only after the zero-shot evaluation. An otherwise identical standard-parameterization sweep provides the primary control.

Here ``128'' denotes the proxy width, not its depth: every MLP remains a plain feed-forward network with \(L=6\). Standard \(\mu\)P does not by itself justify depth transfer. Testing transfer to deeper models would require a separate residual architecture and Depth-\(\mu\)P, which scales both the residual-branch multiplier and the hidden-layer learning rate as \(L^{-1/2}\) in the one-layer-block setting \cite{yang2023tensorprogramsvi}. Because residual connections alter the global correlation dynamics, such a study would be presented as an architectural extension, not as a direct verification of the plain-MLP recursion used in the main theory.

\paragraph{Prespecified comparisons.}
The primary profile contrast is quadratic-early versus its exact quadratic-late reversal; step-early versus step-late is confirmatory. The practical secondary contrast selects one of the two prespecified early profiles from the independent ReLU/SP \(N=256\) confirmation cohort using validation normalized loss AUC, freezes that choice, and compares it with uniform dropout across widths. The optimization contrast is the proxy-selected \(\mu\)P setting versus the transferred standard-parameterization setting. We will report paired mean differences, bootstrap \(95\%\) confidence intervals, exact paired permutation tests, and Holm correction across widths. The pooled profile test averages each seed's paired effect over the four widths; success requires a two-sided exact sign-flip \(p<0.05\), a favorable pooled mean, and a paired-bootstrap upper bound below the prespecified \(+0.01\) test-cross-entropy harm margin at every width. A within-seed interaction per doubling of width is reported but is not itself a gate. For compute-to-baseline, each candidate seed is compared with its paired uniform run's terminal validation-loss threshold at the same width and parameterization. Runs that do not reach it are censored at the training horizon; within a fixed model and width, restricted mean optimizer updates and examples seen are reported together with reach rates. We do not translate these within-model stopping times into approximate FLOP savings.

The \(\mu\)Transfer gate requires the target optimum to remain within one learning-rate-grid interval and transferred loss to lie within \(1\%\) of the target oracle at three of four widths. All four target widths have prespecified oracle sweeps, so the gate is directly evaluable without interpolation.

The complete base allocation is 30 geometry-pilot runs, 100 MLP-confirmation runs, 36 proxy learning-rate runs, 400 zero-shot width-transfer runs, 144 all-width oracle runs, and 30 residual/ViT-bridge runs, for 740 runs total. If leave-one-seed-out proxy selection is unstable, a validation-only 36-run extension is triggered before the 400-run transfer cohort; it is reported separately rather than folded into the base allocation.
