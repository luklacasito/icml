\section{Hermite Spectral Perspective}
\label{sec:var-opt-act}
\label{sec:hermite}

The smooth/kinked distinction can also be read from the activation's Hermite coefficients. For the no-dropout channel at a positive variance fixed point $q^*$, set $f(z)=\phi(\sqrt{q^*}z)$ and assume $f\in L^2(Dz)$. The normalized probabilists' Hermite polynomials $h_n(z)=\mathrm{He}_n(z)/\sqrt{n!}$ form an orthonormal basis under the standard Gaussian measure, so
\begin{equation}
f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n h_n(z),\qquad a_n=\int Dz\,f(z)h_n(z),\qquad \sum_{n\ge0}a_n^2=\int Dz\,f(z)^2.
\end{equation}
For jointly standard Gaussian variables of correlation $c$, this basis satisfies $\mathbb E[h_n(z_1)h_m(z_2)]=c^n\delta_{nm}$; using Lemma~11 of \citet{Daniely2016DualView}, the correlation map becomes
\begin{equation}
F(c)=\frac{\sigma_w^2\sum_{n\ge0}a_n^2c^n+\sigma_b^2}{q^*},\qquad q^*=\sigma_w^2\sum_{n\ge0}a_n^2+\sigma_b^2.
\end{equation}
The coefficient tail determines whether the derivatives needed near perfect alignment remain finite: for each integer $k\ge1$ whose weighted sum converges,
\begin{equation}
F^{(k)}(1)=\frac{\sigma_w^2}{q^*}\sum_{n\ge k}\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}a_n^2.
\end{equation}
Thus the Gaussian derivative moments assumed for the smooth class give the finite-order Taylor expansion used in the Landau equation; convergence of an infinite Taylor series is unnecessary. The smooth example $\tanh$ has rapidly decaying coefficients, whereas \relu{} has a power-law tail for which $F''(1)$ diverges (Fig.~\ref{fig:hermite-decomposition}). The ordinary finite-slope kink, under the regularity assumptions in App.~\ref{app:tube_argument_kink}, produces the $m^{3/2}$ correction in place of the smooth class's $m^2$ term.
