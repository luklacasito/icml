\subsection{Front-Loaded Dropout Across Datasets}
\label{sec:extended_benchmarks}

To test how far the scheduling advantage extends beyond CIFAR, we compare MLPs and Transformers on Speech Commands, Jannis, and Tiny ImageNet, together with a financial MLP on FI-2010. These comparisons span audio, tabular classification, and vision, using the depth-12, width-256 configurations detailed in App.~\ref{app:extended_experiments}. Table~\ref{tab:frontloaded_results} reports the test loss and accuracy of validation-selected models, alongside the final-epoch endpoints where they were recorded.

For each run, we retain the checkpoint with the lowest validation cross-entropy. The reported front-loaded profile was chosen by averaging these minimum validation losses across the original paired seeds, restricting selection to profiles with complete seed coverage. We then froze that profile and its hyperparameters, adding five fresh seed pairs to each five-pair comparison. All checkpoint rows now contain ten pairs; the extended-search Jannis Transformer cohort already had ten and is unchanged. Profiles were not reselected after this extension, and each row keeps the same profile for checkpoint and final endpoints. Final metrics were retained for all ten pairs on Tiny ImageNet 80k, for only the five fresh pairs in the other extended cohorts, and for none in the separate Jannis extended-search cohort.

Speech Commands provides the clearest example of front-loading helping where regularization is already useful. In the original five-seed MLP comparison, uniform dropout reduced checkpoint test loss by $10.82\%$ relative to no dropout. Across the expanded ten-seed comparison, early-step dropout improves on uniform by a further $12.56\%$, with a $95\%$ confidence interval of $[11.36,13.73]\%$, while accuracy increases by $1.72$ percentage points. The Transformer benefits as well: big-step dropout reduces checkpoint test loss by $9.04\%$, with interval $[2.52,15.24]\%$, and improves accuracy by $2.06$ percentage points. These gains are present at the validation-selected checkpoints, before considering any protection against later loss deterioration. Both architectures' loss and accuracy intervals exclude zero. The experiment uses a class-balanced random split of clips from the official training subset, so generalization to held-out speakers remains untested.

Jannis provides a tabular comparison with a different learning problem. Early-step dropout gives the MLP a $2.60\%$ reduction in checkpoint test loss and a $1.56$-percentage-point accuracy gain. The standard Transformer improves loss by only $0.15\%$, while the separate extended-search cohort loses $0.27\%$. Both Transformer loss intervals include zero; the MLP interval is $[1.44,3.74]\%$, leaving the MLP as the clearer signal in this comparison.

On Tiny ImageNet, the selected profiles reduce checkpoint loss by $2.04\%$ and $2.21\%$ for the MLP and ViT at 20,000 training examples, and by $1.17\%$ and $0.76\%$ at 80,000 examples. The larger-data MLP still reaches only $8.00\%$ accuracy, compared with $7.22\%$ for uniform, so its relative gain should be read alongside this weak absolute performance. Its final loss reduction is $8.65\%$, showing how much of the advantage develops after the validation optimum. The larger ViT has an inconclusive checkpoint-loss difference; its final loss improves by $1.66\%$ with interval $[0.51,2.81]\%$, while final accuracy is essentially unchanged.

The FI-2010 MLP improves checkpoint loss by $4.25\%$ and accuracy by $0.81$ percentage points, with both intervals above zero. Its five fresh pairs also illustrate why a lower final loss does not guarantee better classification: big-step dropout reduces final loss by $68.23\%$, from $3.3561$ to $1.0662$, but loses $2.48$ percentage points of final accuracy. The accuracy interval, $[-2.86,-2.09]$ percentage points, is wholly negative. The late-loss effect therefore cannot be read as an accuracy gain or direct evidence against memorization.

These results compare complete training configurations whose learning rates and dropout strengths follow each cohort's validation protocol; mean dropout can therefore differ between the selected profile and uniform. The fixed-budget CIFAR experiments remain the direct control for allocation. The broader evidence shows a useful but task-dependent advantage, with small effects and losses retained alongside the positive cases. App.~\ref{app:extended_experiments} preserves the original multi-profile learning curves and comparisons, including the unrestricted nonuniform results in Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_results}; the main table includes the additional frozen-profile seeds.
