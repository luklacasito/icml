We test these schedules with relatively wide MLPs and Vision Transformers \cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} on CIFAR-10/100 \cite{krizhevsky2009cifar} (App.~\ref{app:experimental_info}). The MLP experiments use modest depth and limited augmentation to study dropout placement. The Transformer experiments test whether the same idea transfers to a different architecture; the MLP initialization theory does not establish their training dynamics. The clearest experimental signal is loss: Table~\ref{tab:loss_improvements} shows final cross-entropy reductions of \(18\)--\(35\%\) in the MLP and activation-sweep settings, with smaller but consistent reductions in the transformer runs. Keeping the same schedules and averaging each seed's minimum recorded test loss gives reductions of only \(0.9\)--\(2.5\%\) across the six comparisons, so the largest differences develop after the loss minimum. This retrospective minimum uses test data to select the epoch; it is separate from the validation-selected checkpoints in the broader benchmark.

Accuracy counts correct labels, while cross-entropy also measures how much probability they receive. In a binary example, reducing the probability assigned to the correct class from $0.8$ to $0.6$ leaves the prediction correct, but increases its loss from $0.22$ to $0.51$. Conversely, increasingly confident mistakes can outweigh improvements on other examples, allowing accuracy and loss to rise together. We therefore report both metrics. On CIFAR-100, the linearly decreasing profile improves final accuracy from $48.61\%$ to $49.27\%$, alongside the loss comparison.

The appendices check that this is not a single-run effect: the gains persist across \(\bar h\)-sweeps and large-width sweeps for kinked \relu{} MLPs (App.~\ref{app:sweeps}, Fig.~\ref{fig:mlp_sweeps}), across the smooth GELU activation class (App.~\ref{app:sweeps}, Fig.~\ref{fig:gelu_h_sweep}), and across transformer schedules and component ablations (Figs.~\ref{fig:dropout_regimes_transformer} and~\ref{fig:ablations}). In these CIFAR experiments, the advantage weakens at high dropout or in narrow networks, consistent with the limitations of the small-field, large-width approximation.

\paragraph{Uncertainty across seeds.}
Tables~\ref{tab:loss_improvements} and~\ref{tab:frontloaded_results} report nominal $95\%$ confidence intervals, pairing runs by their recorded seed or original simulation index. Loss reductions use ratios of seed means, with paired Fieller intervals \cite{fieller1954interval}; accuracy gains use paired Student-$t$ intervals in percentage points. Both use $n-1$ degrees of freedom and assume independent seed pairs with approximately bivariate normal losses or normal accuracy differences, assumptions that three or five seeds cannot establish reliably. We hold the reported schedules and hyperparameters fixed, without adjusting for their selection or for multiple comparisons, so the intervals describe variation across training seeds on the recorded split. They do not include uncertainty from new data or new splits. In the original experiments, the minimum-loss intervals include zero for the three-seed ReLU sweep and the five-seed ViT ablation; the latter's accuracy interval also includes zero. The MLP schedule and budget-control rows share their uniform runs and should not be counted as independent replications.
