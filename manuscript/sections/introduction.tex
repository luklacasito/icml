\section{Introduction}
Deep learning is ubiquitous for its high variance and large degree of overfitting, often containing several orders of magnitude more parameters than classical machine learning counterparts. Additionally, training can reinforce subsets of "internal paths" leading to memorization of training data and greatly reducing the effective parameter count of the model, and significantly reducing the networks potential capability to generalize.
Dropout was formulated to reduce this coadaptation by  corrupting these paths to discourage memorization through independent masks that severe random neurones during the training forward passes. In this setting, how far can useful distinctions propagate through a network that is repeatedly being corrupted? We study this question near the edge of chaos, and ask whether redistributing a fixed amount of dropout across layers can preserve propagation while exposing more of the network to regularization \cite{poole2016exponentialexpressivitydeepneural,schoenholz2017deepinformationpropagation,Bahri2020StatMechDeepLearning,bahri2024leshoucheslecturesdeep}.

Using the representation-group (coarse-graining) language of \cite{roberts2022principlesdeeplearningtheory,bahri2024leshoucheslecturesdeep}, we show that dropout behaves as a relevant perturbation: it displaces the critical fixed point away from perfect correlation and grows under depth coarse-graining, pushing the dynamics away from criticality and determining the macroscopic phase. Concretely, dropout deforms the correlation map \cite{schoenholz2017deepinformationpropagation} by adding a correlation-independent shift at perfect alignment, so that, with independent masks across inputs, perfect correlation is no longer a fixed point for any nonzero dropout. Intuitively this comes from the fact that two signals viewing different dropout masks will always differ in representation as they do not have access to the same number of neurones. This leads to a information propagation- regularization tradeoff, as dropout prevents overfitting at the cost of damaging information propagation.  Thus, even at the edge-of-chaos \cite{sompolinsky1988chaos,packard1988adaptation,bertschinger2004real}, the depth correlation length is finite. While proper information propagation is a valuable proxy for performance \cite{schoenholz2017deepinformationpropagation}, it is not the ultimate objective one is to pursue in the training of neural nets, though it is often the case that all else equal, one will strive to maximize the correlation length, which acts as half life of information propagation. In this work we explore how techniques that hurt information propagation such as dropout, though ultimately aiding generalization and performance, can be optimized through an information propagation lens to minimize the information propagation damage, and empirically show that this leads to better performance compared to standard baselines.


We use Landau Theory from physics to interpret this phase in neural networks drawing similes to other physical systems, and derive an equation of state for the decorrelation order parameter $m \equiv 1-c^\ast$ (with $c^\ast$ the asymptotic inter-signal correlation), derive the resulting power laws, and show that smooth and kinked (\relu{}-like) activations exhibit the same qualitative phase diagram but different critical scaling in the presence of dropout.

Additionally, we show that the two control parameters (dropout strength and distance to criticality) admit a scaling collapse into a single universal form,\footnote{This is analogous to the homogeneous scaling of connected correlation functions near a critical point; related scaling forms hold for one-particle-irreducible vertex functions after the usual Legendre transform \cite{ZinnJustin2002QFTCritical}.} and we illustrate how these predictions compare with mean-field recursions.

The main conceptual contributions are threefold: first, while dropout was previously shown to destroy the order-to-chaos critical point \cite{schoenholz2017deepinformationpropagation}, we show that the deformed mean-field map still retains a nontrivial $c^\ast<1$ fixed point. This allows us to have a well-defined correlation length that can guide architecture depth. Second, we show that universality classes and information propagation scaling laws are set by the analytic structure of activations: smooth and kinked activations have distinct critical exponents (Table~\ref{tab:critical_exponents}), a distinction reflected in their Hermite spectral structure (App.~\ref{sec:var-opt-act}). Since the same Gaussian activation kernels arise beyond MLPs, including CNNs and residual branches (App.~\ref{app:cnn_extension}), we expect the qualitative smooth/kinked split to be a well-motivated heuristic. Third, we treat dropout as a depth-dependent dynamical field; treating classically fixed hyperparameters as fields that can change through the network opens a new dimension for hyperparameter optimization across depth, and potentially over training time using dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) \cite{bordelon2022selfconsistent}.

This work complements prior studies of depth-dependent regularization: stochastic depth drops whole residual blocks with increasing probability \cite{huang2016deep}, curriculum dropout anneals dropout over training time \cite{morerio2017curriculum}, and LayerDrop removes transformer layers for efficiency \cite{fan2019reducing}. Our schedules are spatial depth profiles, so they are complementary to temporal curricula and layer-dropping mechanisms. Qualitatively different edge-of-chaos behavior for smooth and \relu{}-like activations was previously observed in \cite{hayou2019impact}; here we extract the scaling exponents and identify the corresponding universality classes.\footnote{The original notebooks, saved results, and seed-level confidence-interval calculations are available in the \href{https://github.com/luklacasito/icml}{\texttt{icml}} repository. See App.~\ref{app:experimental_info} for the full reproducibility statement.}
